Discolored water in North Arlington: check this first

North Arlington's water comes from the Passaic Valley Water Commission, and the borough sold its own water utility in 2004. When the Commission flushes mains or works on a line, the first sign in a house is often tan or brown water at the tap. That is usually sediment stirred up in the main, not a plumbing failure inside your home.

Run the cold tap in a bathtub for a few minutes. If it clears, the issue was outside. If it stays discolored, or only the hot side is affected, the cause may be your water heater or old galvanized pipe, and that is when a plumber is worth calling. Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in North Arlington

While the crew is on the way, these steps keep a bad leak from getting worse. They apply whether the break is in a kitchen wall or a basement line.

Shut off the main valve

The main shutoff is usually where the water line comes through the foundation, often near the front wall or beside the water meter. Turn it clockwise until it stops.

Kill power to wet areas

If water has reached an outlet, a light fixture or the electrical panel, turn off that circuit first and do not touch wet cords. Call the electric utility if the panel itself is wet.

Clear the area

Take up rugs and move valuables away from the leak. Place a bucket or towels, and keep everyone out of a room where the ceiling is bulging with water.
