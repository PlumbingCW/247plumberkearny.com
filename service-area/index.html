What makes each neighboring town different

Harrison is dense and compact, so a single failure can reach several units. North Arlington's water comes from the Passaic Valley Water Commission, which the borough sold its utility to in 2004. Lyndhurst's township code puts duties on owners for sewer laterals, lead service lines and meter readers. Belleville buys soft water from Newark and sees more freeze-season calls.

Those differences change what we ask on the phone and what we bring. A Lyndhurst call about a house sale starts with the Certificate of Occupancy rules, and a Belleville call in January starts with the main valve and the thaw. Our pages for Harrison, North Arlington, Lyndhurst and Belleville cover each in detail.

Utilities and who to call besides us

Some problems belong to the utility, not a plumber. In Kearny, main breaks and hydrant leaks go to Veolia at (201) 955-7406, and a sewer backup into a house goes to Kearny DPW at (201) 998-3700. Harrison residents can call the Harrison Water Department at (973) 268-2468 for street-side water problems, and Belleville residents can call the Belleville Water Department at (973) 450-3414.

North Arlington customers call the Passaic Valley Water Commission at 973-340-4300. Once the problem is inside your property line, it is yours to fix, and that is when to call us. We say so honestly when the cause sits on the utility side of the meter.

Getting a crew to the right door

Tell the dispatcher your street and the nearest cross street. In dense towns like Harrison and Belleville, a van needs a place to park, and a note about a driveway, a side entrance or a basement door saves minutes. If you live in a multi-unit building, say which unit and whether the building main is shared.

In Kearny, landmarks help. Mention whether you are near Kearny Avenue, the Belleville Turnpike, West Hudson Park or the Arlington side of town. Give the dispatcher whatever is easiest, and the crew will take it from there.

Permits, codes and the town you are in

Permit rules vary by town, so we check before work that needs one. In Kearny, a new or replacement hot water heater needs a zoning permit and a construction permit with the plumbing subcode, and every permit gets an inspection. In Lyndhurst, the township code adds lead service line and sewer lateral requirements that can affect a sale or a renovation.

For anything we cannot confirm for your town, we tell you to ask the local construction code office. That is the honest answer, and it keeps you from relying on a rule that may have changed.
