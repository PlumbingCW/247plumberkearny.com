No heat in winter: what to do first

Check for gas first. Boilers burn fuel, and the first rule is to treat any gas smell as a life-safety issue, not a repair call. Then work through the simple checks while a plumber is on the way.

  1. If you smell gas, leave the building at once, do not flip switches or use a phone inside, and call your gas utility's emergency number or 911 from outside. Call us after the utility says it is safe.
  2. If a carbon monoxide alarm is sounding, get everyone outside into fresh air and call 911.
  3. Check the thermostat is set to heat and above room temperature, and that the boiler switch and the breaker for it are on.
  4. Look at the pressure gauge. A cold boiler usually reads in the range of 12 to 15 psi. Near zero, or very high, tells us what to look for.
  5. Call (973) 488-5410 and give us the make, the fuel, any error light or code, and whether you see water under the boiler.

Do not keep resetting a boiler that keeps locking out. Each reset on a failed ignition can pile up unburned fuel in the combustion chamber.

Why a boiler stops heating

The cause is usually one of a short list. The ignitor or flame sensor is dirty or worn, so the burner will not stay lit. The circulator pump has failed, so hot water never leaves the boiler. A zone valve is stuck. Pressure has dropped because of a leak, or the expansion tank has lost its air charge.

Sometimes the problem is the controls. A low-water cutoff that is dirty will shut the boiler down for safety, and a faulty thermostat or relay can leave a perfectly good boiler idle. Telling those apart takes testing, not guessing, which is why we ask what lights and sounds you see when you call.

Steam, hot water and why they are different jobs

A hot water boiler pushes heated water through the pipes with a circulator pump, and the system is closed and pressurized. A steam boiler boils water into steam, which rises through the pipes by itself and condenses back at the radiators. They look alike in a basement, but they fail in different ways and need different skills.

Steam systems are more sensitive to water level, vent valves and pipe pitch. Hot water systems lean on pump flow, air removal and pressure. Older buildings in this area often have one or the other, and we ask which you have before we arrive.

Gas, carbon monoxide and when a boiler is unsafe

If you smell gas, the answer is the gas utility or 911, not a plumber. If your carbon monoxide alarm sounds, go outside and call 911. A cracked heat exchanger, a blocked flue or a failing burner can produce carbon monoxide, which has no smell.

A boiler that lights with a loud bang, burns with a yellow or lazy flame, leaves soot around the cabinet or shows scorch marks at the draft hood is unsafe to run. We shut it off, explain what we see and do not leave it running if we find a safety failure. In that case a repair may not be the right answer, and we say so.

Boiler work involves gas, venting and pressure vessels. According to the state Uniform Construction Code, a permit is required for plumbing work on existing buildings, and the only exemption is ordinary maintenance such as routine repair, so ask the construction code office about replacements.

Older boilers in an older town

Kearny's mills and housing date to the late 1800s and early 1900s, so older homes are common here, and many of them still run on old cast iron boilers and radiators. That is general knowledge about the era, not a claim about any one building. A well-kept cast iron boiler can run for decades.

The trade-off is parts and efficiency. Fifty-year-old gas valves and controls can be hard to find, and a boiler that needs a major repair each season may be cheaper to replace. Before the cold sets in: a boiler check you can do

A few simple checks in the fall prevent a lot of January calls. None of these require opening the boiler.

  • Run the heat for a few minutes now and confirm every radiator or zone warms up.
  • Look for drips, rust stains or crusty deposits on pipes, valves and the boiler base.
  • Check that the pressure gauge sits in its normal range when the boiler is cold.
  • Keep the area around the boiler clear for air and access, with nothing stored against it.
  • Test your carbon monoxide alarms and replace the batteries.

A yearly service visit cleans the burner, tests the controls and checks combustion. If hot water also runs out, see water heater repair, since the two sometimes share parts of the same system.
