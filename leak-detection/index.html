Emergency Leak Detection in Kearny, NJ

Water stains spreading on a ceiling, a wall that feels warm or damp, or a meter that keeps spinning with every fixture off is an emergency leak detection call, and our 24/7 dispatch line in Kearny, NJ is open at every hour. While you wait, close the main water valve, switch off power to any wet area you can reach safely, and move belongings out of the path of the water.

Leak detection is the process of finding exactly where water is escaping from a pipe, fitting or fixture, using pressure tests, acoustic listening gear, thermal imaging and tracer methods, so the repair opens only the spot that needs opening.

When a hidden leak is an emergency

A slow drip you can see is a repair. A leak you cannot see is a detective problem, and it becomes urgent when water is damaging finished surfaces or when the meter shows a steady flow you cannot account for. Overnight and weekend calls are routine for us, and same-day appointments are available for slower leaks when the schedule allows.

  1. Close the main water shutoff valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house. Turn it clockwise until it stops.
  2. Open one faucet at the lowest level of the house to drain pressure from the pipes, and leave it open.
  3. If water is near outlets, a panel or a ceiling light fixture, turn off power to that area at the breaker, but only if the breaker panel is dry and easy to reach.
  4. Photograph the damage and note when you first saw it. This helps with an insurance claim and helps us trace the path of the water.
  5. Call (973) 488-5410 and tell us what you see, which floor it is on and whether the meter moves with the water off.

If water is coming through a ceiling, poke a small hole to let it drain into a bucket. A ceiling that holds water can fail all at once.

How to confirm you have a leak at all

Before anyone brings equipment, a homeowner can sort a real leak from a misread bill in about ten minutes. Turn off every fixture and appliance that uses water, then look at the meter. Many meters have a small low-flow indicator, often a triangle or dial, that turns when even a trickle passes. If it moves, water is going somewhere.

Then check the usual suspects, because the cheapest leak to find is the one that is not hidden. A running toilet is the most common cause of a high bill. Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank and wait 15 minutes. If color shows in the bowl, the flapper is leaking.

No single method finds every leak. We pick based on pipe material, where the symptom shows and whether the leak is on the pressure side or the drain side.

Leaks behind walls and above ceilings

Water follows gravity and pipe lines, so the stain on the ceiling is rarely directly under the leak. A supply pipe can drip onto a joist, run along it for several feet, and appear at a light fixture far from the source. We trace the stain backward, then confirm with a moisture meter and the listening gear before we cut.

When we have to open drywall, we aim for the smallest access hole that lets us reach the pipe. Many repairs need only a patch about the size of a dinner plate.

Slab leaks and leaks under a basement floor

Pipes under a concrete slab are the hardest to reach. A hot spot on the floor, a sound of running water with everything off, or a damp patch in the middle of a room all suggest a slab leak, and a hot water line is the most likely because heat speeds up corrosion in the soft metal.

Once the line is located, there are three common paths. We can break the slab at the exact spot and repair the pipe. We can reroute the line through the walls or ceiling, which leaves the slab untouched. Or we can replace the whole run. The best choice depends on how many leaks the line has already had and what finishes cover the floor. Pinhole leaks that repeat across a run often point toward repiping that section.

Outside the meter: the service line

A leak between the street and the house is on the service line. In Kearny the Town Water Department operates the distribution system and Veolia handles customer service. For water main breaks, leaks and service disruptions in the street, Veolia's number is (201) 955-7406, answered on weekdays from 8 am to 4 pm and by a 24/7 answering service at night and on weekends.

Ask Veolia where the utility's responsibility ends and yours begins. If the leak is on the private side, we can locate it and recommend water service line repair. The town also posts a lead service line inventory with an address lookup, so check your address before any digging starts.

Once a leak is found, the repair is governed by the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2024 edition, which Kearny's code office says plumbing plans must follow. Opening a wall to reach a pipe is a good time to ask the office whether the repair needs a permit.

Clues that point to a hidden leak

You do not need equipment to notice the early warning signs. If you see any of these, schedule a check before the damage spreads.

  • A water bill that has risen with no change in how many people live in the house.
  • Musty smell in a closet, basement corner or under a sink with no visible water.
  • Paint bubbling, wallpaper peeling or a stain that grows after you run a fixture.
  • The sound of running water when everything is off, or a meter dial that moves.
  • Low water pressure that appeared suddenly in one part of the house.

A burst, as opposed to a slow leak, is a different emergency. If water is gushing, go to burst pipe repair instead.
