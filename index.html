What to do in a plumbing emergency before we arrive

Most of the damage in a plumbing emergency happens in the first hour, and most of it can be limited by what you do in that hour. These steps apply to a burst pipe, a failed water heater and a flooding fixture alike.

  1. Shut off the main water valve. It is usually where the line enters the basement or utility room, and it closes clockwise.
  2. Stop using drains if sewage is backing up. Every gallon you add has nowhere to go.
  3. Switch off power to wet areas only if you can reach the panel without standing in water. Keep children and pets away.
  4. Move belongings out of the water path and put down towels or a bucket if a ceiling is dripping.
  5. Call (973) 488-5410 and say which fixture or pipe is involved. The dispatcher uses that to send the right crew and parts.

If the problem is in the street or at the water meter, the water utility handles it. Kearny's water department contractor takes main breaks and hydrant leaks at (201) 955-7406, and a sewer backup into a house should also go to Kearny DPW at (201) 998-3700.

Why plumbing fails in older Kearny homes

Kearny became a township in 1867 and a town in 1899, and the mill-era streets near the old Clark Thread and Nairn Linoleum sites still hold plenty of older houses. As a general matter, homes built before about 1960 often have galvanized supply pipe, cast iron drains or clay laterals, and those materials corrode, scale and crack with age. We confirm what you have with a camera or a visual check rather than assuming.

Kearny also sits between the Passaic and Hackensack rivers, with Meadowlands wetlands to the east. Low-lying property drains slowly after heavy rain, which is why sump pumps, backwater valves and clear laterals matter here. Our sump pump service and sewer camera inspections are built around those conditions.

Kearny water, sewer and permits: who handles what

Kearny's water comes from the North Jersey District Water Supply Commission, with the town's water department running the distribution system and Veolia handling billing and customer service. If your tap water looks wrong, or there is a main break outside, start with Veolia at (201) 955-7406, and a recorded line answers on nights and weekends.

On the sewer side, the town clears blockages in its own main and the homeowner is responsible for the lateral that runs from the house to it. That split is why an emergency plumber and Kearny DPW often get called on the same night.

Permits matter too. A new or replacement hot water heater needs a zoning permit and a construction permit with the plumbing subcode, and every permit gets an inspection. Ask the Kearny construction code office about your specific job, because permit rules vary.
