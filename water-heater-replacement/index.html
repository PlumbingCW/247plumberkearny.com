When a failing water heater is an emergency

A tank that leaks from its body does not get better. The steel liner has corroded through, and the flow only grows. A leak is an emergency, and so is a heater that smells of gas. Do these things in this order.

  1. If you smell gas, leave the building at once and call your gas utility or 911 before you call us. Do not flip switches or light anything.
  2. For an electric heater, turn off its breaker at the panel. For a gas heater, turn the gas control knob to off or pilot, if no gas smell is present.
  3. Close the cold water shutoff valve on the pipe feeding the top of the tank. A lever turned across the pipe, or a wheel turned clockwise, stops the supply.
  4. Open one hot tap upstairs so the tank can vent as it drains, and do not touch water that has been heated, because it can scald.
  5. Call (973) 488-5410. Tell us gas or electric, how big the tank looks and where it sits, so we can bring the right unit.

If the leak is at a pipe fitting on top instead of from the tank body, the heater may be repairable. We check that first on every call.

Signs the tank has failed for good

Water at the base is the clearest sign, but check where it comes from. A drip at the relief valve or a top fitting is often a separate repair. Water seeping from the jacket or pooling beneath the tank with no pipe above it means the steel shell has rusted through.

Other signs show up earlier. Rust-colored hot water, a rumbling or popping sound as sediment cooks at the bottom, and hot water that runs out far sooner than it used to all point to a tank near the end of its life. Many tanks last roughly 8 to 12 years, and age is a good reason to replace instead of repair.

Choosing the replacement: size, fuel and fit

The best replacement is usually the same fuel and a similar size, because that keeps the gas line, vent or electrical circuit unchanged. Switching fuel is possible but adds work, such as a new circuit, a new vent or a gas line.

Size is about people and habits, not just gallons. A household with several people showering in the morning needs a bigger first-hour supply than a couple. Tell us how many people live in the house and when hot water is used most, and we will match a tank to that instead of copying the old label.

What we check before the new tank goes in

Replacing the tank is the easy part. The surrounding items are what make an install safe and legal.

  • The condition of the shutoff valve, because a seized valve can fail when we close it.
  • The flexible water connectors and the gas connector, which are replaced with new ones.
  • The temperature and pressure relief valve and its discharge pipe, which must run down toward the floor.
  • The vent on a gas unit, including slope, draft and signs of backdrafting or corrosion.
  • Whether the house needs an expansion tank, a drain pan or a new sediment trap on the gas line.

We show you anything that fails this list and explain why before we add it to the work.

What a replacement visit involves

After shutting off power or gas and water, we drain the old tank to a floor drain or sump with a hose. We disconnect the water lines, the gas or electric supply and the vent, and we remove the old unit. A tank full of water is heavy, so draining first matters.

The new unit is set in place, level, on a pan where one is needed. We connect the water with new flexible lines, add the relief valve and discharge pipe, connect the gas or power, and check for leaks. Then we fill the tank, purge the air and test the heating before we leave.

Permit and inspection for a new water heater in Kearny

Kearny's construction code office states that a new or replacement hot water heater needs a zoning permit and a construction permit with the plumbing subcode. All permits require an inspection. Plumbing work in the state must follow the National Standard Plumbing Code, and a licensed plumber must do the work.

The town says small jobs typically take about 7 business days to process, with up to 20 working days allowed. Because processing time varies, ask the construction code office at (201) 955-7880 how it handles an urgent replacement. We pull the permit and arrange the inspection so it does not fall on you.

Hot water while you wait and water that damages the tank

Until the new tank is in, you can heat water on the stove for washing. Do not run the failed unit. A leaking electric element dry-fired is a hazard, and a gas unit with a damaged vent is a risk.

Water chemistry affects how long a tank lasts. Hard water builds sediment and shortens life. Kearny buys its water from the North Jersey District Water Supply Commission, and the town's 2024 report lists no hardness figure, so ask the water utility if you want your own number. In neighboring Harrison, the Passaic Valley Water Commission reported about 84 to 100 ppm, which falls in the moderately hard range.

Older basements, tight closets and replacement in Kearny homes

Many Kearny houses date to the late 1800s and early 1900s, and the tank is often in a low basement corner near a floor drain, or in a cramped utility closet. Those spots matter. A basement heater that sits on the floor of a room prone to flooding does better raised on a stand.

In older homes, the supply piping to the tank may be galvanized, and it can crumble when disturbed. We tell you before we start if the connections look fragile, and we can replace the short section with copper or PEX.
