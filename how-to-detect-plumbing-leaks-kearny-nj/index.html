How to Detect Plumbing Leaks at Home in Kearny, NJ

By the Kearny 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 13, 2026.

A plumbing leak is water escaping from a supply line, drain or fixture, and the sneaky ones stay hidden behind walls, under slabs or in toilets that look fine. The tests below find most of them without opening anything.

Start with the free checks, then move to professional methods if the leak is still a mystery.

Start with the water meter test

The meter is your most honest leak detector. Turn off every fixture, appliance and ice maker in the house, then find the meter and look at the leak indicator. It is often a small triangle or dial that spins when any water moves.

If it moves with everything off, water is going somewhere. Write down the reading, wait an hour without using water, then read it again. A change means a leak between the meter and your fixtures.

The meter test, step by step

Follow this sequence so the result means something.

  • Shut off all water use, including the washer, dishwasher and the ice maker.
  • Make sure no toilet is running and no one flushes during the test.
  • Find the meter, lift its cover if needed, and note the digits and the small leak dial.
  • Wait at least an hour, ideally two, without touching any tap.
  • Read the meter again. Any change confirms a leak.
  • Close the main shutoff valve and read again after the wait. If the dial stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps moving, the leak is between the meter and the shutoff.

A moving meter with the main closed points to the service line. See water service line repair for how those leaks are located and fixed.

Toilet leaks and the dye test

A silent toilet leak can waste a lot of water. Drop a few drops of food coloring or a dye tablet into the tank and wait 15 minutes without flushing. If color appears in the bowl, the flapper or fill valve is leaking.

Flappers wear, harden and warp over time, and mineral buildup keeps them from seating. Replacing a flapper is a simple fix. If the dye stays put but the meter still moves, look elsewhere.

Moisture meters, thermal cameras and listening gear

A pin or pinless moisture meter reads the dampness of drywall, wood and tile. A thermal camera shows temperature differences, which can reveal a hot line running through a cold slab or a wet spot cooling the surface. Neither one gives a final answer by itself, since condensation and sun can fool them.

Acoustic gear listens. A ground microphone or a contact listening device picks up the hiss of pressurized water escaping from a pipe. A plumber closes valves, listens along the route and narrows the leak to a few feet before cutting.

Pressure tests and tracer gas

A pressure test isolates a section of pipe, fills it and watches a gauge. If the pressure falls, the section leaks. This works well for supply lines and for finding which leg of a branch is bad.

For hidden leaks that sounds and heat miss, a tracer gas mix of hydrogen and nitrogen can be injected into an isolated line. A sensor at the surface finds where the gas escapes. It is a professional method that avoids tearing up finished floors. Our leak detection work uses these tools to avoid unnecessary demolition.

When a leak is also a safety issue

If water touches electrical panels, outlets or appliances, turn off the power at the breaker only if you can reach it dry, and call a professional. A ceiling that bulges with water can collapse. Shut the main valve and, if possible, drain the pipes by opening low taps.

Slow leaks are less dramatic but cause mold and rot over time, so do not wait for a stain to spread.

What to do once you find the leak

Shut off water to the fixture or the main, take photos of the damage for your records, and dry the area quickly. Fans and open windows help, and wet insulation or drywall often has to be removed to prevent mold.

Call a plumber for repairs that require opening walls or replacing pipe. Homes in North Arlington and Harrison often have older supply piping, and a leak in one spot can signal wear elsewhere. If several leaks appear, discuss repiping rather than patching.
