Tankless Water Heater in Kearny, NJ

A tankless water heater warms water only when a tap is open, so there is no tank to keep hot and no tank to run out. Done well, it is a good fit for many Kearny homes. Done badly, it gives you lukewarm showers and an expensive gas bill.

We plan these jobs on paper first: how much hot water you really use, where the unit can vent, and what the gas line or electrical panel can carry. You get a clear price before work starts.

How an on-demand heater makes hot water

When you open a hot tap, a flow sensor in the unit sees water moving and fires a gas burner or switches on electric elements. Cold water passes through a heat exchanger and leaves hot. Close the tap and the unit shuts off.

The key number is temperature rise, the gap between incoming water and the temperature you want. Cold winter water coming in needs a bigger rise than summer water, so the same unit delivers fewer gallons per minute in January than in July. Good sizing is done for the coldest month, not the average one.

Sizing by flow rate, not by gallons

A tank is sized by how much it holds. A tankless unit is sized by how many gallons per minute it can heat to the right temperature while several fixtures run together. Add up what could run at the same time, such as a shower, a dishwasher and a kitchen tap, and match that to the unit's rating at your temperature rise.

Undersizing is the most common mistake. A unit that is fine for one shower may fade when a second bathroom starts up. If your house has a large soaking tub or body sprays, tell us up front, because those fixtures can eat most of a small unit's output on their own.

What has to be ready before a tankless unit goes in

The unit itself is the easy part. These are the items that usually decide the scope of the job.

  • Gas line capacity. Gas tankless units draw much more fuel at once than a tank heater, and an old half inch line often has to be upsized.
  • A vent path to the outside. Condensing units use plastic pipe through a wall, non-condensing units need stainless or special-grade vent.
  • A condensate drain for condensing models, since they produce acidic water that must be neutralized and drained.
  • Electrical supply. Even gas units need an outlet, and electric tankless models can need heavy dedicated circuits that a small panel cannot give.
  • Space on the wall with working clearance around the unit and an accessible shutoff valve on each line.

If the gas line or panel needs work, we say so in the plan before anything is ordered. A cheaper unit that needs an expensive utility upgrade is not always the better deal.

Gas, electric and what each asks of your house

Gas tankless units give the highest flow rate and are the usual choice for a whole house. They need venting, a gas line sized for the burner and combustion air. Electric units have no flue, which makes them easy to place, but a whole-house electric model draws a lot of power and may need a service upgrade.

Small electric point-of-use units suit a single sink or a far-off bathroom. They do not replace a whole-house heater, but they can shorten the wait for hot water at a fixture that is a long pipe run from the main unit.

Hard water and keeping the heat exchanger clean

Minerals in water come out as scale when heated, and a tankless heat exchanger has narrow passages that scale can choke. Symptoms are a falling flow rate, error codes and uneven temperature. The fix is descaling, which means circulating a mild acid solution through the unit using isolation valves.

Those valves are installed with the unit so flushing takes minutes instead of a service call. How often you descale depends on your water. Ask your water utility for the hardness figure, or have us check. A sediment filter at the inlet protects the unit from grit after nearby main work.

Kearny water and permits for a tankless install

The Town of Kearny Water Department buys water from the North Jersey District Water Supply Commission, and the town's 2024 report lists distribution pH between 7.0 and 8.0 but no hardness figure. Because water quality varies by supplier and zone, we do not assume. Neighbors on the Passaic Valley Water Commission system, like North Arlington, have a published hardness range that is moderately hard.

A new or replacement hot water heater in Kearny needs a zoning permit and a construction permit with the plumbing subcode, and every permit requires an inspection. Gas piping and electrical work can add their own subcodes, so ask the construction code office what applies. We prepare the paperwork and schedule the inspection with you.

According to Kearny's construction code page, plumbing plans must follow the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2024 edition. Gas, vent and electrical requirements for a tankless unit are separate, so ask the construction code office which subcodes apply.

Fitting a tankless unit into an older Kearny home

Many Kearny houses and apartment buildings, like those in neighboring Harrison, date to the early 1900s, and older homes often have galvanized or cast iron lines and tight basements. That is a general pattern, not a rule for every address, but it means we check what the new unit will connect to.

Galvanized supply pipe full of scale can clog a tankless inlet filter, so we may recommend replacing the first stretch of pipe. In a two-family house with a shared basement, the vent route and the gas meter location often decide where the unit can sit. Water heater installation with a standard tank is the simpler path if a safe vent cannot be found.

When a tankless heater is not the right answer

Be skeptical of any contractor who says tankless is right for every home. A household that runs many fixtures at once, a house with no safe vent route, or a gas supply that cannot be upsized sensibly may be better served by a new tank. If you plan to leave in a few years, the payback may never arrive.

If your tank unit has already failed, our water heater replacement service gets hot water back quickly, and you can revisit tankless later. We would rather explain the trade-offs than sell you the wrong unit.
