Harrison water and what it does to your plumbing

Harrison's Water Department, reachable at (973) 268-2468, buys most of its water from the Passaic Valley Water Commission. The Commission's Little Falls plant measured hardness between 84 and 100 ppm in 2023, which is about 5 to 6 grains per gallon and sits in the moderately hard band of the USGS scale.

Moderate hardness is slow to scale a fixture but it builds up inside a tank water heater over the years. If your heater is older than about eight years and has never been flushed, scheduling water heater repair before it fails in January is cheaper than an emergency swap.

The town's 2023 water report lists lead testing from 2021 with a 90th percentile of 1.76 ppb against a 15 ppb action level. Lead mostly comes from service lines and interior plumbing, not the supply, so the report's advice applies: flush the tap for 30 seconds to 2 minutes if water has sat for hours, and ask the water department about your service line.

Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Harrison

The next ten minutes matter more than the next ten hours. These four steps limit damage in a Harrison building while the crew is on the way.

Shut off the main valve

Look where the water line enters the basement or utility room, often on the street-facing wall. In a multi-unit building, find out who controls the building main, and if the break is outside, call the Harrison Water Department at (973) 268-2468.

Kill power to wet areas

If water is near outlets, a panel or an appliance, switch off that circuit at the breaker, but only if you can reach the panel without standing in water.

Clear the area

Move rugs, boxes and electronics out of the path of the water, and lift furniture legs onto blocks if you can do it quickly. Keep children and pets away from standing water.

The services Harrison properties ask about most

In older, tightly built blocks, drains and sewer laterals are the usual trouble. Our emergency drain cleaning clears sink, tub and floor drain clogs, and when sewage backs up in a basement we follow with a camera look at the line.

Hidden leaks are the second pattern.
