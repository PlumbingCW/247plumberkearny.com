Main Sewer Line Repair in Kearny, NJ: What Affects the Cost

By the Kearny 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 13, 2026.

A main sewer line repair fixes the pipe that carries waste from your house to the street sewer. Quotes differ widely because the cost follows depth, length, access and method, not just the pipe.

This guide explains each driver, how the town's responsibility split works and what to ask before approving work.

Which pipe we mean by main sewer line

People use the phrase two ways. The street sewer main belongs to the town or a sewer authority. The pipe that runs from your house to that main is the lateral, and it is usually what a plumber means when repairing a main sewer line at a house.

Knowing which one is failing decides who pays and who does the work. A camera inspection shows where the problem is and whether it sits inside your property.

Who is responsible in Kearny

The Kearny DPW says that when sewage backs up into a house, you call DPW at (201) 998-3700. DPW checks the main sewer line. If the main is flowing, the blockage is in the lateral, and the property owner must hire a plumber. If the main is blocked, the town clears it.

The property owner is responsible for maintaining and repairing the lateral sewer line. That is why a camera inspection matters before a big repair: it shows whether the damage is on your side of the connection.

The method often changes the price more than the length does. Each has strengths and limits.

How depth and length affect the work

The deeper the pipe, the more soil must be removed and shored. Trenches past a certain depth need protective measures, which adds time and equipment. Length matters because every additional foot means more pipe and more digging or lining.

A short run with a single break behaves very differently from a full lateral that fails in multiple places. We aim to find the damage first, then price only the scope that actually needs repair.

What the camera inspection should tell you

Before a quote, you want a recording of the inside of the pipe. A good inspection answers these questions.

  • Where the pipe is damaged, shown as a distance from the cleanout.
  • What the damage is, such as a crack, a root intrusion, a sag or an offset joint.
  • What the pipe is made of, because cast iron, clay and plastic fail differently.
  • Whether the line holds water in a low spot, called a belly.
  • Where the property line or the connection to the street main falls.

Our sewer inspection service records the line so you can see the damage and share it with a second contractor if you want another opinion.

In the Meadowlands District and South Kearny commercial areas, wastewater collection belongs to the Kearny Municipal Utilities Authority, which has five pumping stations and 24/7 emergency staff.

In Lyndhurst, the township code requires an owner of new construction or a major residential renovation to replace the sewer lateral and connect to the street sewer main. Older pipe and why the failures happen

Homes built before about 1960 often have cast iron or clay laterals. Cast iron scales and corrodes from the inside. Clay joints let roots in, and settling shifts pipe out of line. Plastic pipe fails less from age, but poor bedding or a crushed section can still cause a belly.

Kearny's mills and housing date back to the late 1800s and early 1900s, so older homes are common. We check the actual pipe before assuming. Sometimes cleaning on a schedule is the better choice than a full replacement.

Getting a second opinion without wasting time

If a quote seems high, ask for the camera footage and a written scope. A fair contractor will share it. Compare the method, the length covered and what restoration is included, not just the total.
