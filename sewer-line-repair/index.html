Emergency Sewer Line Repair in Kearny, NJ

Sewage coming up through a basement floor drain, several fixtures backing up at once or a lawn that is soaked and smells like a sewer is an emergency sewer line repair call, and our 24/7 dispatch line in Kearny, NJ answers at every hour. While you wait, stop using water in the house, keep people and pets away from the wastewater, and call Kearny DPW to check the town's main.

Sewer line repair means fixing a broken, collapsed, offset or root-damaged drain pipe between your house and the street main, by digging to the break or by relining the pipe from the inside.

Sewer backup: what to do first

A sewer backup is a health hazard as well as a mess, because raw wastewater carries bacteria. We take these calls any hour of the night or weekend, and same-day appointments are available for non-urgent sewer problems such as a slow main or an odor.

  1. Stop using every water source. No flushing, no laundry, no dishwasher, no showers. Every gallon you send down goes to the lowest drain in the house.
  2. Keep children, pets and anyone with a weakened immune system out of the area. Wear rubber boots and gloves if you must enter, and do not touch the water with bare hands.
  3. Turn off the power to any basement area with standing water only if you can reach the panel without wading. Otherwise call the electric utility.
  4. Call Kearny DPW at (201) 998-3700. They check the town's main. If the main is flowing, the blockage or break is in your lateral. If it is blocked, the town clears it.
  5. Call (973) 488-5410 and tell us which fixtures are backing up and whether DPW found the main clear.

Do not pour chemical drain cleaner into a backed-up line. It will not fix a broken pipe and it makes the line more dangerous to open.

Clog or break: how to tell

A clog and a broken pipe look alike at first, because both send water back up. The difference shows in the pattern. A clog opens with a cable and stays open for months. A break, a collapse or a bad offset returns within days or weeks, because the cable clears the blockage but the damaged pipe catches new debris.

A camera settles it. We feed a small waterproof camera down the line from a cleanout, watch the video and mark the depth and the position of any defect. Then we show you what we saw, so the repair decision is based on the pipe itself.

Repair methods: dig, line or burst

Sewer repair has more options than most homeowners expect. The right choice depends on what the camera shows, how deep the line is and what sits above it.

A liner follows the shape of the old pipe. If the line has a belly that holds water, lining preserves it. A camera and a slope check tell us which method fits.

Why sewer lines fail

Roots are the best-known cause. Tree roots grow toward the moisture and nutrients in a sewer line and enter through any joint or hairline crack. Once inside, they thicken and catch debris. Clay pipe has many short sections with many joints, which makes it a favorite for roots.

Other causes include cast iron that rusts and flakes from the inside, bituminous fiber pipe (often called Orangeburg) that softens and deforms, ground that settles and offsets the joints, and heavy loads above shallow pipe. Homes built before about 1960 may have any of these, though the camera is the only way to know what yours has.

Who owns the sewer lateral in Kearny

Kearny's DPW is clear on this. If sewage backs up into a house, they check the town's main. If the main is flowing, the blockage is in the lateral, and the property owner is responsible for maintaining and repairing the lateral sewer line.

In some low-lying parts of town, waste will not flow by gravity and sanitary pumping stations lift it instead. In the Meadowlands district and the South Kearny industrial areas, the Kearny Municipal Utilities Authority handles wastewater collection, with its own 24/7 emergency staff, at 973-465-5367. A property there may need KMUA instead of DPW.

Next door, Lyndhurst's township code requires owners doing new construction or major renovation to replace the sewer lateral and connect to the street main. See our Lyndhurst plumbing page for that area.

Sewer repairs follow the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2024 edition, which Kearny's code office says plumbing plans must follow, and permit rules vary, so ask the office before digging starts. Every permit receives an inspection before the trench is closed.

Warning signs before a full backup

Sewer lines rarely fail without warning. If you notice any of these, a camera check now is cheaper than an emergency later.

  • Gurgling toilets or drains when another fixture is used, a sign the line is restricted.
  • Slow drains in more than one fixture, especially on the lowest floor.
  • Sewer smell in the basement or yard with no visible cause.
  • A sunken or unusually green patch of lawn along the path of the line.
  • Repeated clogs that return after cabling, which suggests a damaged pipe instead of a one-time blockage.

For roots or grease that need to come out before a camera can see, sewer line cleaning is usually the first step.

Permits, inspection and keeping it honest

Under the NJ Uniform Construction Code, plumbing work on an existing building needs a construction permit. The only listed exemption is ordinary maintenance, which includes routine repair. A new or relined sewer lateral is generally more than routine repair, so ask the Kearny construction code office. Kearny's page says all permits require an inspection.

We do not recommend digging a line that a liner can fix, and we do not recommend lining a line that has collapsed. If a spot repair or regular cleaning on a schedule will carry you for years, we say so. The camera video is yours to keep for the repair decision and the permit file.

While you wait: protecting the house

If wastewater reached the floor, keep the room ventilated and keep children away. Nights and weekends are the most common time for backups, because that is when the house uses the most water. Move belongings off the floor and avoid carrying sewage-wet items through clean areas.

Do not run the washing machine to see if the line has cleared. If you have a sump pump or ejector pit, check that it is not discharging into the sanitary line, because that is prohibited in many towns. Our drain cleaning page covers the clog side of backups.
