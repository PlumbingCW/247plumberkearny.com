Lyndhurst's code and what it means for your plumbing

The Township of Lyndhurst's sewer and water chapter spells out several things a property owner has to do. A new water connection needs a written application and fee, and the township may refuse a connection that was not built to its plumbing code. Service connections must be laid at least three and a half feet deep to stay below the frost line.

For a house sale or a new occupant, proof of lead service pipe replacement, or an exclusion, is required before a Certificate of Occupancy. A new owner must also have an outside remote meter reader installed through a licensed plumber before final plumbing and occupancy approvals. Check with the township on timing, because these items can delay a closing.

The code also says that for new construction or a major residential renovation, the owner must replace the sewer lateral and connect it to the street main. That is a large job, and it is better priced and scheduled before a contractor opens the walls.

Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Lyndhurst

Lyndhurst homes vary a great deal in age, so we start with the same four steps everywhere. Do them while the crew is on the way.

Shut off the main valve

If the leak is on your side of the meter, closing the main stops the flow. A newly installed outside remote meter reader can also help you find the meter and the shutoff quickly.

Kill power to wet areas

Turn off any circuit that serves a wet room, and unplug nothing while standing in water. If a basement outlet is submerged, leave the breaker off and stay out.

Clear the area

Move belongings out of the path, especially on a basement floor, and keep the area around the sump pit clear so a crew can reach it.
