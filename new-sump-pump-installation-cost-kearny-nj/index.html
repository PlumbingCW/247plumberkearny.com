New Sump Pump Installation in Kearny, NJ: What Affects the Cost

By the Kearny 24/7 Plumbing team. Published October 13, 2026.

A new sump pump installation includes a pit, a pump, a check valve, a discharge line and power. The price changes mostly with what the job needs, such as digging through a slab or adding battery backup, not with the pump brand on the box.

Here is what drives the cost, so you can compare quotes on the same basis.

What a new installation includes

A complete job starts with a pit. If the basement has no pit, the slab is cut, soil is dug out and a liner is set so water collects there. Then a pump is placed inside on a firm base, and a vertical discharge pipe with a check valve carries water out through the wall.

The pump needs its own outlet, ideally on a ground-fault circuit interrupter (GFCI) circuit, because it sits in water. The final step is a test, where the pit is filled with water to confirm the float switch turns the pump on and off cleanly.

Submersible or pedestal: which pump fits

The two common pump designs suit different basements, and the choice affects both price and life span.

FeatureSubmersiblePedestal
LocationSits inside the pit, under waterMotor mounted on a column above the pit
NoiseQuiet, because the pit muffles itLouder
Pit sizeNeeds a deeper, wider pitFits a narrow pit
Typical lifeOften shorter if run constantly, but can be replaced easilyMotor is out of water and can last longer
CostUsually costs moreUsually costs less

Wet basements with finished space generally favor submersible pumps. Tight utility pits often use pedestals.

Sizing the pump and pit

Pump size is not about brute power. It is about how much water must be moved and how high it must be lifted. Horsepower and lift height are balanced against how often water fills the pit, which depends on soil, drainage and the home's footing drains.

A pump that is too small runs nonstop, and one that is too large cycles in short bursts that wear the switch. A plumber estimates by looking at the pit, the discharge height and how quickly water refills. Ask whether your quote includes the pit or only the pump.

Questions to ask before you accept a quote

Two quotes can look far apart because they cover different scopes. Ask each contractor these questions.

  • Does the price include the pit liner and the slab cut, or only the pump?
  • Is a check valve included, and where does it sit?
  • Where will the water go, and does that outlet comply with local rules?
  • Is there a backup, such as a battery or water-powered system, and what does it add?
  • Who pulls the permit, and is the inspection included in the price?
  • What warranty applies to the pump and the labor, in writing?

For comparison with a repair job instead of a new unit, read about sump pump installation and repair to see when a new unit is worth it.

When replacing is better than repairing

A pump that is several years old, cycles often and shows rust on the housing may be near the end of its life. Replacing it while the weather is dry costs less than an emergency swap during a storm, when the basement is already flooding.

A pump that is only stuck on a float or has a failed check valve is usually repairable. Honest advice means telling you when the cheaper fix is the right one.

Backup power and the second pump

A pump only helps while it has power. Storms that flood basements also cut electricity. A battery backup pump has its own float, battery and charger, and takes over when the main pump fails or the power drops. A water-powered backup uses municipal water pressure, though it uses water and may be restricted in some areas.

Backup systems add to the price, but they also protect finished basements and stored belongings. The battery must be tested and replaced on schedule. A backup alarm that signals a high water level is cheap insurance.

Kearny, Lyndhurst and where the water goes

Kearny sits beside the Passaic and Hackensack rivers, with Meadowlands wetlands to the east, and low-lying property drains slowly after heavy rain as a general matter. Some low-lying parts of town depend on sanitary pumping stations because waste will not flow by gravity.

The Lyndhurst township code says stormwater and unpolluted drainage may not go into the sanitary sewer, which affects where a sump line can end. For Kearny, ask the construction code office about discharge rules and permits, because permit rules vary. Plumbing work on existing buildings needs a construction permit under state rules, with rough and final inspections on one- and two-family homes.

As a general electrical guideline, a sump pump should be on a GFCI-protected circuit. Ask the construction code office whether a new pit or discharge change needs a permit.

Installation day and aftercare

Expect noise if a slab is cut, dust, and a few hours without a working sump while the pump is swapped. If you can, clear the area around the pit and let the plumber know where the breaker is.

After installation, test the pump every few months by pouring a bucket of water into the pit. Clean the intake screen, check the float moves freely, and listen for odd sounds. Homes in Belleville and Lyndhurst share similar low ground concerns, and basement waterproofing can reduce the load on the pump.
