Toilet overflowing: stop the water first

A toilet overflow gets worse every few seconds, and the first minute decides how far the water travels. These steps stop it. Night or weekend, we answer the same line, and same-day appointments are available for toilets that are failing but not flooding.

  1. Take the lid off the tank and push the rubber flapper down to the bottom, which stops water entering the bowl.
  2. Turn the supply valve on the wall or floor behind the toilet clockwise until it stops. That cuts the water to the tank.
  3. Do not flush again. Each flush adds more water to a bowl that is already full.
  4. Lay towels around the base and keep children and pets away. If the water is dirty, treat it as contaminated.
  5. If water is coming up in the tub or the floor drain too, stop using every fixture and call us at (973) 488-5410 and Kearny DPW at (201) 998-3700.

If the supply valve will not turn, shut the main valve where water enters the house.

Reading what the toilet is telling you

Most toilet trouble falls into a few patterns. Water at the floor around the base points to a failed wax ring or a loose closet bolt, not a cracked bowl. Water dripping from the tank bottom means a worn tank gasket or loose tank bolts. A tank that keeps refilling is leaking past the flapper.

A bowl that rises slowly and drains with a gurgle has a clog in the trap or the line beyond it. Knowing which one you have saves a wasted trip, so describe the symptom when you call.

Why a toilet rocks and what happens to the floor under it

A rocking toilet is more than an annoyance. Every shift squeezes the wax ring a little, and eventually it lets water or sewer gas past. Slow leaks at the base soak the subfloor, which in older bathrooms can soften plywood or rot boards around the flange.

When we pull a toilet, we look at the subfloor and the flange. A flange that sits below the finished floor, or one that is cracked or corroded, needs a repair ring or a replacement before the toilet goes back. Skipping that step is why some toilets leak again within a year.

Clogs: plunger, auger and when to stop

A flange plunger, the kind with a rubber sleeve, works better on toilets than a flat cup. Seal it over the drain hole and push and pull with steady strokes. A closet auger reaches farther and does not scratch the porcelain, which a coat-hanger will.

If the water does not drop after a few honest tries, stop. Forcing it can push a clog deeper or crack the trap. A toilet that clogs again and again may have a main line problem, so see our guide to drain cleaning to learn what repeated backups mean.

Signs a toilet should be replaced instead of repaired

A repair is the better answer for most toilets, and we will say so. These are the situations where replacement makes more sense.

  • A crack in the bowl or tank, even a hairline, that weeps water.
  • A glaze that has worn rough, trapping waste so the bowl never looks clean.
  • A toilet that clogs often despite a clean drain, a sign of poor flush design.
  • Parts that are no longer made, so every repair is a workaround.
  • Water use that bothers you, since older tanks use much more per flush than current models.

For a new unit, we haul away the old toilet, set a new wax ring and check for leaks at the supply and base. If you also want a new fixture elsewhere in the room, see plumbing installation.

When a toilet backup is really a sewer problem in Kearny

If the toilet backs up and the tub or a basement floor drain does too (a pattern we also see in Harrison basements), the problem is likely beyond the bowl. Kearny's town guidance says to call DPW at (201) 998-3700 to check the main sewer line. If the main is flowing, the blockage is in your lateral, and the property owner has to hire a plumber.

That is the point where a toilet repair call turns into a drain job. We will tell you which one it is on the phone if we can, and on site if we cannot, so you do not pay for a toilet repair on a line problem.

Setting a new toilet is plumbing work governed by the National Standard Plumbing Code, 2024 edition, according to Kearny's code office. Replacing a fixture in the same spot is often simple, while moving it raises permit questions.

Installing a new toilet: what the job involves

A new toilet is not just a swap. We shut off the supply, drain the tank, disconnect and lift the old bowl, scrape off the old wax, inspect the flange, install a new wax ring or seal, set the new bowl, tighten the closet bolts evenly and connect a new supply line.

Fit matters. Toilets have a rough-in distance, measured from the wall to the center of the drain, and the new bowl has to match. Round or elongated bowls and standard or comfort height are choices we will go over, along with the flush type, so the toilet works with your drain and your space.

Overflowing toilet: what to do while you wait

If the overflow has stopped, leave the supply valve closed and use another bathroom. Mop up with towels and open a window to help the floor dry. Do not run a fan across wet sewage, since it spreads spray.

If the toilet is only running or hissing, closing the supply valve stops the waste of water and gives you time to schedule. Our dispatch can usually set up a same-day visit when the schedule allows, and the after-hours line is open if it gets worse overnight.
